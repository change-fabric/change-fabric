# Lenses

Three fixed lenses run every time. A fourth, the topic lens, is chosen at framing.

## user-value

Generate from the side of the person who lives with the result. What would make the pain in the framing go away, what would they notice first, and what would they stop having to do? Favor options that change the experience, even when they cost more to build.

## simplest-thing

Generate the smallest options that could plausibly work. What can be reused, configured, or deleted instead of built? What is the version that ships this week? Favor options with the fewest moving parts and the least new surface.

## risk-and-ops

Generate from the side of whoever runs it and is paged for it. What fails, how is it noticed, how is it undone? Favor options that are reversible, observable, and safe to roll out in steps, and name the operational cost of each.

## Choosing the topic lens

The topic lens covers the axis the framing makes most uncertain and the fixed lenses do not. Pick it from the hard constraints and what success looks like. Examples:

- **data model**: when the shape of stored state is the real decision.
- **migration path**: when existing users, data, or callers must move across.
- **developer ergonomics**: when the main users are engineers calling an API or tool.
- **cost**: when spend, quota, or paid calls bound the design.

Offer two or three candidates at the framing question, the best fit first. Write its brief in the same shape as the fixed ones: whose side it generates from and what it favors.
